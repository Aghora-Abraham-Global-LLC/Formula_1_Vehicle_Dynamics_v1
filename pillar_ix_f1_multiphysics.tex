\subsection{Singularities in Classical Vehicle Multibody Formulations}
In conventional vehicle dynamics implementations \cite{Pacejka2012, Milliken1995}, tire slip quantities are defined kinematically:
\begin{equation}
  \kappa = \frac{\omega_{\mathrm{wheel}} R_e - v_x}{\max(v_x, \epsilon_v)}, \qquad \tan\alpha = -\frac{v_y}{\max(|v_x|, \epsilon_v)},
  \label{eq:classical_slip_singular}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is longitudinal slip, $\alpha$ is lateral slip angle, $\omega_{\mathrm{wheel}}$ is wheel angular velocity, $R_e$ is effective rolling radius, and $(v_x, v_y)$ are hub contact-point velocities. When a vehicle undergoes low-speed maneuvers, hairpin corner apexing, or standing starts ($v_x \to 0$), the denominator in~\eqref{eq:classical_slip_singular} forces standard ODE integrators to either divide by zero or encounter extreme numerical stiffness induced by the arbitrary threshold parameter $\epsilon_v$, creating artificial non-conservative damping.

Concurrently, vertical tire-road contact and suspension bump-stop engagements exhibit structural stiffness discontinuities:
\begin{equation}
  F_z = \begin{cases}
    k_{\mathrm{tire}}\,\delta_z + c_{\mathrm{tire}}\,\dot{\delta}_z, & \delta_z > 0, \\
    0, & \delta_z \le 0,
  \end{cases} \qquad
  F_{\mathrm{bump}} = k_{\mathrm{linear}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{susp}} + k_{\mathrm{bump}}\left(\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{susp}} - \delta_0}{\delta_{\max} - \delta_{\mathrm{susp}}}\right)^{\!p},
  \label{eq:bumpstop_singularity}
\end{equation}
where $k_{\mathrm{bump}} \ge 5000\text{ kN/m}$ and $p \ge 2$. Under violent curb strikes at $300\text{ km/h}$, $\delta_{\mathrm{susp}} \to \delta_{\max}$, triggering a vertical acceleration spike ($\sim 40\text{G}$) that causes variable-step integrators to collapse step size below $\Delta t < 10^{-9}\text{ s}$ or crash outright.

\begin{proof}

\subsection{Ground-Effect Venturi Aerodynamics and Underfloor Boundary-Layer Detachment}
In the ground-effect regulatory regime, aerodynamic downforce is dominated by twin underfloor Venturi tunnels. Under quasi-steady conditions, total aerodynamic downforce $F_z^{\mathrm{aero}}$ and pitching moment $M_y^{\mathrm{aero}}$ are nonlinear functions of front and rear ride heights $(h_f, h_r)$, pitch angle $\theta = (h_r - h_f) / L_{\mathrm{wheelbase}}$, roll angle $\phi$, and yaw angle $\beta$:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
As vehicle velocity $v_\infty$ increases along a straight, downforce scales quadratically ($F_z \propto v_\infty^2$), compressing the suspension until the floor ride height reaches a critical proximity to the track surface ($h_f \to 5\text{--}8\text{ mm}$). The diffuser stalls abruptly, causing an instantaneous collapse of $C_L$. With aerodynamic suction removed, the stored mechanical strain in the suspension heave springs violently pushes the chassis upward. As ride height increases ($h_f > 15\text{ mm}$), the adverse pressure gradient relaxes, airflow reattaches, downforce re-engages, and the chassis is sucked back toward the ground. This self-excited aeroelastic instability is **porpoising**.

\subsection{Unsteady Reduced-Order Model (ROM) and Hopf Bifurcation Analysis}
To model porpoising without intractable real-time 3D Navier--Stokes execution, we couple the 2-DOF chassis heave ($z$) and pitch ($\theta$) structural equations to an internal aerodynamic flow lag state $\Gamma_{\mathrm{aero}}(t) \in [0, 1]$ representing the diffuser flow attachment fraction:
\begin{equation}

  \label{eq:aero_flow_lag}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{flow}} \approx \frac{L_{\mathrm{floor}}}{v_\infty} \approx 15\text{--}25\text{ ms}$ is the convective transit time, and $\mathcal{S}$ is a smooth sigmoid describing static separation hysteresis. The dynamic downforce is:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
